================================================================================
          ASSEMBLY PROTOCOL: PHYSICAL CHASSIS INTEGRATION MATRIX
================================================================================

This document outlines the final mechanical steps required to lock your hardware 
boards, pogo-pin arrays, and co-extruded TPU components into a single, unified 
benchtop simulation node.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MECHANICAL PARTS MATRIX & CHASSIS SEATING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CLEAN THE CORE: Pull your printed base plate chassis off the printer bed. 
   Inspect the interior 42.5-degree channels to verify they are completely 
   clear of stringing or stray plastic loops.
2. PRESS-FIT THE PINS: Take your pre-soldered gold pogo pins and press them 
   directly into the structural base sockets. The sockets are designed with an 
   interference fit; press until the tips protrude exactly 2.0mm through to 
   contact your internal conductive TPU loops.
3. SECURE THE MOUNT: Mount the Xilinx Artix-7 FPGA board onto the integrated 
   chassis riser tray. Lock it down using four M3 x 6mm nylon screws to isolate 
   the silicon circuit board from generating localized static interference.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INITIAL TEST RUNWAY: FIRST POWER-ON DIAGNOSTIC CHECKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before connecting your main USB logic analyzer, execute these sequential safety 
checks to protect your hardware chips from unexpected voltage shorts:

- CHECK 1: THE CONTINUITY AUDIT (UNPOWERED)
  * Tool: Digital Multimeter set to Continuity mode (Beep test).
  * Action: Probe between individual pogo-pin terminals and your common ground 
            ring. The multimeter must stay completely silent. 
  * Target: If it beeps, a copper wire line is shorted. De-solder immediately.

- CHECK 2: STATIC SIGNAL RESISTANCE VALUE CORES
  * Tool: Digital Multimeter set to Resistance (Ohms).
  * Action: Measure between your active prime pogo pins and the ground circuit.
  * Target: You should read a steady, finite resistance value matching the 
            density profile of your Lubrizol conductive carbon TPU loops. 

- CHECK 3: INITIAL LOW-VOLTAGE COMMENCE IGNITION (POWERED)
  * Action: Connect your dedicated USB programming cable from your computer to 
            the Artix-7 board power port. Flip the master switch to ON.
  * Target: Confirm that the green power LED stays lit and steady, and that 
            no internal components generate excessive thermal heat spikes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DATA ROOM SYNCHRONIZATION EXECUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Once the hardware passes all three diagnostic checks, launch your terminal:
1. Load your compiled constraints file `quantum_hardware_pins.xdc` into your 
   Vivado environment to program the hardware gates.
2. Boot up your automated unit test suite `test_quantum_core.py` to confirm your 
   virtual Linux pipeline passes with green checkmarks.
3. Open your Saleae Logic Analyzer software to read the outbound telemetry 
   pulses coming from your PMOD JB header.
================================================================================
